Abstract. The main result of the paper is the existence of an infinitely many families
of Ramanujan-type congruences for b4 (n) and b6 (n) modulo primes m ≥ 2 and m ≥ 5,
respectively. We provide new examples of congruences for b4 (n) and b6 (n). Moreover,
we find two infinite explicit infinite families of congruences for b4 (n) modulo 3.

In 1919, Ramanujan discovered three remarkable congruences for the unrestricted
partition function p(n), as shown below:
p(5n + 4) ≡ 0 (mod 5),
p(7n + 5) ≡ 0 (mod 7),
p(11n + 6) ≡ 0 (mod 11).
We refer to such congruences as Ramanujan-type congruences. Lovejoy [7] studied
the distribution of b2 (n) and proved the existence of Ramanujan-type congruences for
b2 (n) modulo every prime m ≥ 5. In a previous work, the author [15] demonstrated
similar results for b3 (n) and b5 (n).
The primary result of this paper is as follows:
Theorem 1.1.
(1) For every prime m ≥ 2, there exist infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences
of b4 (n) modulo m.
(2) For every prime m ≥ 5, there exist infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences
of b6 (n) modulo m.
Specifically, we find two infinite explicit families of congruences for b4 (n) modulo 3.

5. Open problems
In this paper, we establish the existence of infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences modulo m for b6 (n), where m ≥ 5. Additionally, congruences modulo 3 have
been explored (see [1]). However, the question arises: what occurs when m = 2?
Problem. For b6 (n), either discover a Ramanujan-type congruence modulo 2 or prove
that no such congruence exists.
While Ramanujan-type congruences modulo nearly all primes m do exist, it is
important to note that discovering them may require extensive computations. We
encourage readers who are interested to explore and seek examples of congruences
modulo different primes.
